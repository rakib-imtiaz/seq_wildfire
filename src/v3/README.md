**Design Read:** Reading this as: a proposal-led B2B landing page for BC utility and industrial buyers, with a calm architectural-studio language (a painted terrain model on a white base under soft daylight), leaning toward hand-written CSS + one scroll-pinned three.js maquette + restrained GSAP snapping.

# v3 "Maquette"

The main page since 2026-10-21: served at `/` (`index.html` → `src/v3/main.tsx`; it was `/v3/`). The form stays at `/proposal` (the v1 app) and the earlier design is at `/v1/`. Plan: `docs/v3-plan.md`. Shared rules: `docs/versions-brief.md`.

## Dials (taste-skill §1)
- `DESIGN_VARIANCE 6`: asymmetric but calm; an architect's presentation board, not an agency site.
- `MOTION_INTENSITY 6`: one authored moment (the pinned maquette), quiet reveals elsewhere.
- `VISUAL_DENSITY 4`: airy, but each beat carries a short fact sheet (the user: "the side text too minimal").

## Deliberate overrides of the taste skill
- **Light only, no dark mode.** The plan locks a light studio; the whole concept is daylight on a scale model.
- **No icon library.** The brief forbids installing anything, so the 4 glyphs (arrows, play, pause) are authored SVG, one 1.5 px stroke.
- **Google Fonts `<link>`.** The brief mandates it (shared rule).
- **Hand-written CSS, no Tailwind.** make_3d_website: "the CSS is part of the craft"; Tailwind is not installed.

## The 3D (one canvas, one WebGL context)
- **One scale for everything (2026-10-20 rework).** The user's note: "the physics, the object shape, nothing is matching". The first build put the real-size M3T over a 1:250 model, so the drone read as an 87 m aircraft, twice its own thermal footprint, and the stepped boards threw the footprints onto vertical walls. Now the land, the infrastructure, the forest and the drone are ALL 1:250, so every size, height and footprint is true to the others.
- **Not cartoony (2026-10-21).** The user, three times: "the frame looks soo cartoony the tree models also looks cartoony", "too cartoony". The white plaster land with smooth white cone trees read as a toy up close. Now the land is painted (dry open ground, forest floor under the stands, rock on steep faces, gravel roads, matte slate water), the trees are the v1 table's four species, the pylons are lattice steel and the buildings concrete. The cut sides and the base stay white (a painted terrain model on a white base). The v1 rule holds here too: procedural trees look toy-like close up, so the coverage and heat beats frame from further out (`fill` 0.5 to 0.64).
- **Model:** the shared fictional watch zone (`/table/js/zone.js`, 1600 × 1000 m) at 1:250 (6.4 × 4.0 m of "studio") as one milled surface from the shared height field (`src/lib/terrain.ts`), with contour lines engraved by the shader (10 m, index 50 m; faint on the model, inked in for the map). The reservoir is a basin at 30 m (14 m under the dam crest) with a low bank on the designed shoreline.
- **Forest:** 22k instanced conifers (30k on `high`): Douglas-fir, spruce (cooler north-facing slopes), lodgepole (a few rust and grey snags) and ponderosa (dry south-facing benches). Star-sectioned tiers, per-tree tint and stand-scale colour patches, random placement over the forest density (no planted grid). The SAMPLE hotspot is a smouldering stump in a small canopy gap, as in the report's imagery. Trees carry the lens footprints and the thermal like the ground does.
- **Drone:** the real M3T build (`/table/js/m3t/model.js`, metres) scaled by 1:250. It hovers 77 m out from the ~37° slope along its normal (`optics.js` STANDOFF), which is 100 m above the ground under it, heading and gimbal squared to the slope. Rendered in a second pass (layer 1) with its own shadow-casting copy of the key light, framed on the 0.4 m aircraft. Close beats are macro shots of it; from the coverage beat on it is a point at the apex of its own field of view, marked by a quadcopter map symbol (`Labels.tsx`, not to scale) and an "M3T, 100 m above ground" plate.
- **Optics:** each lens is a real perspective camera with FACTS §3 FOVs (wide 84°, tele 15°, thermal 61° DFOV, read as diagonal; 4:3, 4:3, 5:4). Its frustum is projected onto the surface, the trees and the massing in the shader. W / T / IR toggles isolate one cone. The coverage beat's footprint figures (111 × 83, 16 × 12, 71 × 57 m) come from the same optics and STANDOFF (`data/copy.ts`).
- **Heat (2026-10-21):** the land is never painted with ironbow (a magenta sheet over the trees read as "so un professional"). The heat beat shows what the thermal lens sees as an inset: the engine renders the land from the lens's own camera with the thermal materials (`kit.thermal`: cool crowns, warmer ground, cold water, the 312 °C core) into a target and composites it top-right; `components/ThermalFrame.tsx` draws its frame (lens, Iron Red, low gain, a spot meter, the palette scale). On the model the footprint is a cased cobalt outline over the canopy (`OUTLINE_IR`).
- **Professional pass (2026-10-21):** the main site's type system (Barlow Condensed caps for headings and buttons, Barlow, JetBrains Mono for labels and data), square buttons, 3 px radii; a chapter number over each beat; titles without full stops. Cut sides show strata (topsoil, subsoil, till, rock beds, water in section). Cut blocks are irregular polygons with young regrowth. Routes have a pale casing and show only in the team and map beats (`ROUTES`); the team beat looks from the east, down both routes.
- **Camera:** each beat frames a box; across a big zoom the frame's centre travels with its real size, so the aircraft stays in view through the pull-back. Haze (fog to the studio colour) in the close beats only.
- **Side copy:** every beat has a fact sheet under its line (`components/BeatFacts.tsx`, all from FACTS.md). Phones under 960 px tall hide it in the pinned story (the picture stays big); the motion-off story shows it everywhere.
